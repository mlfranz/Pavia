5.2a BENEFICIARY -- University of Pavia (UNIPV), Italy
(max 1 page)
----------------------------------------------------------------

General description:
The University of Pavia (UNIPV), founded in 1361, is one of Europe's
oldest and most prestigious higher education institutions, organised
into 18 Departments with some 24,000-26,400 students and roughly 1,000
faculty members (recognised by the European Commission as a Jean Monnet
European Centre of Excellence). UNIPV will participate in this project
through the Bioinformatics, Mathematical Modelling and Synthetic
Biology (BMS) laboratory (http://lab-bioinfo.unipv.it), part of the
Department of Electrical, Computer and Biomedical Engineering. Over the
last 20 years, the BMS lab has become one of the leading European
groups in modeling and simulation to support drug development,
combining mathematical modeling (including PK/PD and PBPK modeling),
data mining, model identification and computer science, closely linked
to UNIPV's Master's programme in Bioengineering and PhD programme in
Bioengineering, Bioinformatics and Health Technologies.

Role and profile of supervisor:
Prof. Paolo Magni (Full Professor of Bioengineering, UNIPV; PhD in
Bioengineering, 1998) is Director of the BMS Lab and has taught
mathematical modeling of biological systems, physiology, statistics,
bioinformatics and pharmacometrics since 2005. Author of more than 400
publications (160 in international journals); co-founder of two
spin-off companies and co-founder of the DDMoRe Foundation, which
originated from the FP7-IMI DDMoRe project that he co-led as UNIPV PI
and work-package leader. Former associate editor, IEEE Transactions on
Biomedical Engineering. Chair of the Bioengineering Bachelor's and
Master's degree programmes and Erasmus delegate; vice-chair of the PhD
programme in Bioengineering and Bioinformatics. Since 2004 he has
supervised 42 PhD students (several awarded the Italian national prize
for best Bioengineering PhD thesis) and 16 postdoctoral researchers,
including 4 PhD students hosted from other universities/countries, with
international collaborations spanning Pamplona, Paris, Berlin, Athens,
London, Leiden, Valencia and Porto Alegre. He has consulted for major
pharmaceutical companies (including GSK, Janssen, Chiesi, Merck Serono,
Menarini, Italfarmaco, Nerviano Medical Science, Servier, Angelini and
Pharmacia) on PK/PD population modeling, PBPK modeling, and
Bayesian/MCMC estimation methods. This would be his first MSCA-funded
fellow (confirmed directly by him), extending an already substantial
doctoral/postdoctoral supervision record to this specific scheme.

Key research facilities, infrastructure and equipment:
PCs, servers and modeling/simulation software (NONMEM, Monolix, PK-Sim,
MATLAB, R, Simulix, Python); access to a computing cluster hosted by
UNIPV's Department of Mathematics; and access to the CINECA Leonardo
EuroHPC supercomputer, one of Europe's largest HPC systems -- all
confirmed directly by Magni as available to this project.

General description: (sourced from icsi.berkeley.edu and Wikipedia --

ICSI is an independent, non-profit 501(c)(3) research institute founded
in 1988, based in Berkeley, California, and an Affiliated Institute of
the University of California, Berkeley. Its position adjacent to but
administratively independent from UC Berkeley gives researchers ready
access to campus collaborators and resources, without teaching or
committee obligations. ICSI is internationally recognised for
pioneering research in computer networking, speech and language
processing, computer vision, machine learning and AI, and usable
security and privacy, among other areas. Since 1988, it has hosted more
than 600 visiting postdoctoral researchers and senior scientists,
reflecting a long-standing institutional commitment to international
research exchange -- directly relevant to this project's outgoing
phase.
