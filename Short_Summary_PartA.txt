================================================================
Generative AI increasingly designs drug candidates, but workflows rely on static descriptors and computational proxies for potency and safety, not on how a molecule behaves in vivo. Physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) modeling predicts real exposure and concentration-time behavior, but is used only as a downstream check after generation.

During a DAAD-funded stay at ICSI Berkeley, the applicant audited three PARP1-targeted generative pipelines with a PBPK simulation layer. Despite passing every conventional static filter, a meaningful share of shortlisted candidates had no pharmacokinetically achievable dose at all, roughly 70% could not be confidently classified as safe once uncertainty was propagated, and about 85% were each outperformed on potency and safety by an alternative conventional scoring had not selected.

This project moves PBPK from a downstream check to an optimization signal. During an 18-month outgoing phase at ICSI, a trainable surrogate will connect generative design directly to PBPK-simulated exposure, optimizing molecules against time-resolved concentration-time profiles rather than single-point estimates, extended to JAK2 and, in the return phase, a third target, testing generalizability across three target families rather than one. During a 12-month return phase at Pavia, under Prof. Paolo Magni, population PK/PD and nonlinear mixed-effects expertise will add inter-individual variability to the optimization objective -- a dimension the preliminary single-individual audit could not capture.

Expected outcomes: a framework integrating generative AI with mechanistic PBPK modeling, an account of where conventional potency/safety objectives fail to predict PBPK-confirmed outcomes, and open, reproducible workflows at the intersection of generative AI, drug discovery and quantitative pharmacology -- an independent research profile and a template for making pharmacokinetics explicit in AI-driven drug design from the start.
